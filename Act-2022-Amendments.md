# CITANZ Constitution — Amendments for Compliance with the Incorporated Societies Act 2022

**Prepared for:** Chinese IT Association New Zealand Incorporated (CITANZ)

**Date:** 5 March 2026

**Purpose:** Draft new and amended Rules to comply with the Incorporated Societies Act 2022 (NZ) for re-registration

---

## [NEW SECTION] Section 9A — Officers' Duties and Disqualification

*This new section is required to comply with sections 45, 47, 49–57, and 62–73 of the Incorporated Societies Act 2022, which impose statutory duties on officers, prescribe disqualification criteria, and require conflict of interest procedures.*

**9A.0 Interpretation**

For the purposes of this Section:

(a) "Officer" means a member of the Committee and any other person occupying a position in the Society that allows them to exercise significant influence over the management or administration of the Society (for example, a chief executive, treasurer, or general manager), as defined in section 2 of the Incorporated Societies Act 2022.

(b) "Act" means the Incorporated Societies Act 2022.

**9A.1 Composition of the Committee — Majority Membership Requirement**

A majority of the Officers on the Committee must be members of the Society, or representatives of bodies corporate that are members of the Society, in accordance with section 45(3) of the Act.

**9A.2 Qualifications of Officers**

(a) Every Officer must be a natural person.

(b) A person may be elected or appointed as an Officer only if that person:

&emsp;(i) has consented in writing to be an Officer; and

&emsp;(ii) has certified that they are not disqualified from being elected or appointed or otherwise holding office as an Officer of the Society.

**9A.3 Disqualification**

The following persons are disqualified from being elected or appointed, or otherwise holding office, as an Officer of the Society:

(a) a person who is under 16 years of age;

(b) a person who is an undischarged bankrupt;

(c) a person who is prohibited from being an officer or promoter of, or being concerned or taking part in the management of, an incorporated or unincorporated body under the Companies Act 1993, the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, or the Takeovers Act 1993;

(d) a person who is disqualified from being an officer of a charitable entity under section 36C of the Charities Act 2005;

(e) a person who has been convicted of any of the following offences, and has been sentenced for the offence, within the last 7 years:

&emsp;(i) an offence under the Incorporated Societies Act 2022;

&emsp;(ii) a crime involving dishonesty (within the meaning of section 2(1) of the Crimes Act 1961);

&emsp;(iii) an offence under section 143B of the Tax Administration Act 1994;

&emsp;(iv) an offence, in a country, State, or territory other than New Zealand, that is substantially similar to an offence specified in subparagraphs (i) to (iii);

&emsp;(v) a money laundering offence or an offence relating to the financing of terrorism, whether in New Zealand or elsewhere;

(f) a person who is subject to a banning order under subpart 7 of Part 4 of the Act, or any substantially similar order under the law of another jurisdiction as prescribed by regulations;

(g) a person who does not comply with any additional qualifications for Officers contained in this Constitution.

**9A.4 Duties of Officers**

Every Officer of the Society must, when exercising powers or performing duties as an Officer:

(a) act in good faith and in what the Officer believes to be the best interests of the Society;

(b) exercise any power as an Officer for a proper purpose;

(c) not act, or agree to the Society acting, in a manner that contravenes the Act or this Constitution;

(d) exercise the care and diligence that a reasonable person with the same responsibilities would exercise in the same circumstances;

(e) not permit, cause, or allow the Society's activities to be carried on in a manner likely to create a substantial risk of serious loss to the Society's creditors; and

(f) not agree to the Society incurring any obligation unless the Officer reasonably believes the Society will be able to perform the obligation.

**9A.5 Conflict of Interest — Disclosure Obligation**

(a) An Officer who is interested in a matter relating to the Society must disclose the nature and extent of the interest (including, where possible, the monetary value of the interest) to the Committee as soon as practicable after the Officer becomes aware of the interest.

(b) For the purposes of this rule, an Officer is "interested in a matter" if:

&emsp;(i) the Officer, or a relative of the Officer, may obtain a financial benefit from the matter; or

&emsp;(ii) the Officer, or a relative of the Officer, has a financial interest in a person to whom the matter relates; or

&emsp;(iii) the Officer is otherwise interested in the matter because this Constitution says so.

(c) An Officer is not interested in a matter merely because:

&emsp;(i) they receive indemnity, insurance cover, remuneration, or other benefits authorised under the Act;

&emsp;(ii) the Officer's interest is the same or substantially the same as the interest of all or most other members of the Society by reason of their membership; or

&emsp;(iii) the Officer's interest is so remote or insignificant that it cannot reasonably be regarded as likely to influence the Officer in carrying out their responsibilities.

**9A.6 Conflict of Interest — Consequences**

(a) An Officer who is interested in a matter must not:

&emsp;(i) vote or take part in any decision of the Committee relating to the matter; or

&emsp;(ii) sign any document relating to the entry into a transaction or the initiation of the matter.

(b) An Officer who is interested in a matter may still take part in discussions relating to the matter and be present when the Committee makes a decision, unless the Committee decides otherwise.

(c) If all non-interested Officers consent, an interested Officer may participate in the vote or decision or sign the relevant document.

(d) If half or more of the Officers are interested in a matter and therefore cannot vote, the Committee must call a Special General Meeting to determine the matter, in accordance with section 64(3) of the Act.

**9A.7 Interests Register**

The Committee must keep and maintain an Interests Register recording all disclosures made under Rule 9A.5. The Interests Register may be inspected by any Officer at any reasonable time.

## [NEW SECTION] Section 9B — Committee Structure, Appointment, Roles, and Meetings

*This new section is required to comply with sections 26(1)(e) and 26(1)(f) of the Incorporated Societies Act 2022, which require the constitution to set out the manner in which officers are elected or appointed, the quorum and procedure for committee meetings, and voting procedures.*

**9B.1 Composition of the Committee**

The Society shall have a Managing Committee ("the Committee") comprising:

- President
- Secretary
- Treasurer
- Such other Members as the Society shall decide at a Society Meeting.

Only Members of the Society may be Committee Members. The President, Secretary, and Treasurer are Committee Members and Officers of the Society. There shall be a minimum of three (3) Committee Members, as required by section 45(1) of the Act.

**9B.2 Election and Term of Committee Members**

At a Society Meeting, the Members may decide by majority vote:

- The size of the Committee;
- Who shall be the President, Secretary, and Treasurer;
- Whether any Committee Member may hold more than one Officer position;
- The length of the Term for each Committee Member.

Committee Members hold office until the conclusion of the next Annual General Meeting unless otherwise decided.

**9B.3 Nomination Procedures**

Nominations for Committee Members shall be called for at least 28 days before the Annual General Meeting.

Each candidate must be proposed and seconded in writing by Members, and nominations must be delivered to the Secretary no later than 5pm on the fifth day before the AGM.

All retiring Committee Members are eligible for re‑election.

**9B.4 Vacancies**

If the position of any Officer becomes vacant between Annual General Meetings, the Committee may appoint another Committee Member to fill the vacancy until the next AGM.

If the position of any Committee Member becomes vacant, the Committee may appoint another Member to fill the vacancy until the next AGM.

A Committee Member who is absent from three consecutive Committee Meetings without leave may have their position declared vacant by the President.

**9B.5 Cessation of Committee Membership**

A person ceases to be a Committee Member if:

- They resign by written notice to the Committee;
- They are removed by majority vote at a Society Meeting;
- Their Term expires.

A person who ceases to be a Committee Member must return all Society documents and property within one month.

**9B.6 Role of the Committee**

The Committee is responsible for:

- Administering, managing, and controlling the Society;
- Carrying out the purposes of the Society;
- Managing the Society's financial affairs;
- Approving annual financial statements;
- Setting accounting policies;
- Delegating responsibilities and co‑opting Members as necessary;
- Ensuring Members follow the Rules;
- Managing membership processes;
- Setting meeting dates and agendas;
- Managing complaints procedures;
- Setting membership fees;
- Making regulations.

The Committee has all powers of the Society unless limited by these Rules or by a majority decision of the Members.

**9B.7 Roles of Officers**

**President**

Responsible for ensuring compliance with the Rules, convening and chairing meetings, overseeing operations, and reporting at the AGM.

**Secretary**

Responsible for minutes, the Register of Members, records, correspondence, and filing documents with the Registrar.

**Treasurer**

Responsible for accounting records, preparing financial statements, reporting at the AGM, and providing financial information to the Committee.

**9B.8 Committee Meetings**

Committee Meetings may be held in person or via electronic means.

A quorum is more than half of the Committee Members.

Decisions are by majority vote, with the President holding a casting vote.

Only Committee Members present (including by electronic means) may vote.

Meetings may be adjourned if necessary, and adjourned meeting rules follow standard Society procedures.

## [NEW SECTION] Section 9C — Financial Benefit and Pecuniary Profit

*This new section is required to comply with sections 22–24 of the Incorporated Societies Act 2022, which provide that a society must not be carried on for the financial gain of its members, and to set out permitted payments.*

**9C.1 No Private Pecuniary Profit**

No member or Officer of the Society may derive any private pecuniary profit from membership of the Society, except as permitted under this Constitution or the Act.

**9C.2 Permitted Payments**

The Society may:

(a) reimburse members or Officers for reasonable expenses properly incurred on behalf of the Society;

(b) pay reasonable remuneration to a member or Officer for services provided to the Society, provided that:

&emsp;(i) the payment is reasonable and relative to market rates;

&emsp;(ii) the member or Officer is not involved in the decision to approve the payment; and

&emsp;(iii) the payment is recorded in the Interests Register.

**9C.3 Conflict of Interest**

Any financial benefit must comply with the conflict of interest rules in Section 9A.5 and 9A.6.

## [NEW SECTION] Section 9D — Contact Person

*This new section is required to comply with sections 26(1)(h) and 112–113 of the Incorporated Societies Act 2022, which require the constitution to set out how the contact person will be elected or appointed.*

**9D.1 Appointment**

The Committee shall appoint at least one (1) Contact Person for the Society. The Contact Person must be a person who is eligible to be an Officer of the Society, is at least 18 years of age, and is ordinarily resident in New Zealand, in accordance with section 112 of the Act.

**9D.2 Responsibilities**

The Contact Person is responsible for communications with the Registrar of Incorporated Societies and for maintaining the Society's contact details on the register.

**9D.3 Replacement**

The Committee may replace the Contact Person at any time and must notify the Registrar of any change as soon as practicable.

## [AMENDED] Section 12 — Admission of Members

*This section is amended to include an express requirement for member consent, as required by section 26(1)(c) of the Incorporated Societies Act 2022.*

**12.0** The following classes of member are entitled to be admitted to membership of the Association:

(a) Full Member — any natural person, company, firm, or incorporated or unincorporated body engaged in, or having an interest in, Information Technology in New Zealand;

(b) Student Member — any natural person who is enrolled in a tertiary institution in New Zealand; and

(c) Honorary Member — any person whom the Committee considers has rendered distinguished services to Information Technology or to the Association.

**12.1** Applications for Full Membership and Student Membership shall be made in writing (including by electronic means) to the Secretary and shall include:

(a) the applicant's name, contact details, and such other information as the Committee may reasonably require; and

(b) **a clear statement of consent confirming that the applicant consents to becoming a member of the Association and agrees to be bound by this Constitution and any bylaws of the Association.**

**12.2** Each application for membership shall be considered by the Committee at its next meeting, or by any subcommittee or process established by the Committee for that purpose. The Committee may, in its absolute discretion, approve or decline any application for membership. The Committee is not required to give reasons for declining an application.

**12.3** Honorary Members may be nominated by any Full Member and shall be elected at a General Meeting by a resolution passed by a majority of those present and voting.

**12.4** Every member, upon admission, shall be deemed to have consented to comply with and be bound by this Constitution and any bylaws of the Association as amended from time to time.

**12.5** The Committee shall ensure that the Register of Members is kept up to date and includes such information as is required by section 79 of the Incorporated Societies Act 2022.

## [NEW SECTION] Section 14A — Dispute Resolution Procedures

*This new section is required to comply with section 26(1)(j) and sections 38–44 of the Incorporated Societies Act 2022. These procedures are modelled on Clauses 2–8 of Schedule 2 of the Act. If the Society includes these provisions in its Constitution, the procedures are presumed to be consistent with natural justice under section 41 of the Act.*

**14A.0 Application**

(a) This Section applies to disputes between any combination of:

&emsp;(i) members of the Society;

&emsp;(ii) Officers of the Society; and

&emsp;(iii) the Society itself,

where the dispute relates to an allegation that a member, an Officer, or the Society:

&emsp;(A) has engaged in misconduct;

&emsp;(B) has breached, or is likely to breach, a duty under this Constitution, any bylaws, or the Incorporated Societies Act 2022; or

&emsp;(C) has damaged the rights or interests of a member, or of members generally.

(b) Nothing in this Section limits any person's right to apply for an order, or take any other enforcement action, under Part 4 of the Incorporated Societies Act 2022.

**14A.1 How a Complaint is Made**

(a) A member or an Officer may make a complaint by giving to the Committee (or any complaints subcommittee appointed by the Committee under Rule 14A.6) a notice in writing (including by email or other electronic means) that:

&emsp;(i) states that the member or Officer is starting a procedure for resolving a dispute in accordance with this Constitution;

&emsp;(ii) sets out the allegation to which the dispute relates and identifies the person against whom the allegation is made (the "Respondent"); and

&emsp;(iii) sets out any other information reasonably required by the Society.

(b) The Society may make a complaint against a member or an Officer by giving to the member or Officer a notice in writing that:

&emsp;(i) states that the Society is starting a procedure for resolving a dispute in accordance with this Constitution; and

&emsp;(ii) sets out the allegation to which the dispute relates.

(c) The information given under Rules 14A.1(a)(ii) or 14A.1(b)(ii) must be sufficient to ensure that the Respondent is fairly advised of the allegation concerning them, with enough detail to enable them to prepare a response.

(d) A complaint may also be made in any other reasonable manner permitted by the Committee.

**14A.2 Right of the Complainant to be Heard**

(a) A member or an Officer who makes a complaint ("the Complainant") has a right to be heard before the complaint is resolved or any outcome is determined.

(b) If the Society makes a complaint:

&emsp;(i) the Society has a right to be heard before the complaint is resolved or any outcome is determined; and

&emsp;(ii) an Officer may exercise that right on behalf of the Society.

(c) Without limiting how the Complainant may be given the right to be heard, the Complainant must be taken to have been given the right if:

&emsp;(i) the Complainant has a reasonable opportunity to be heard in writing or at an oral hearing (if one is held);

&emsp;(ii) an oral hearing is held if the Decision Maker considers that an oral hearing is needed to ensure an adequate hearing;

&emsp;(iii) any oral hearing is held before the Decision Maker; and

&emsp;(iv) the Complainant's written statement or submissions (if any) are considered by the Decision Maker.

**14A.3 Right of the Respondent to be Heard**

(a) The Respondent has a right to be heard before the complaint is resolved or any outcome is determined.

(b) If the Respondent is the Society, an Officer may exercise the right to be heard on behalf of the Society.

(c) Without limiting how the Respondent may be given the right to be heard, the Respondent must be taken to have been given the right if:

&emsp;(i) the Respondent is fairly advised of all allegations concerning them, with sufficient details and time given to enable the Respondent to prepare a response;

&emsp;(ii) the Respondent has a reasonable opportunity to be heard in writing or at an oral hearing (if one is held);

&emsp;(iii) an oral hearing is held if the Decision Maker considers that an oral hearing is needed to ensure an adequate hearing;

&emsp;(iv) any oral hearing is held before the Decision Maker; and

&emsp;(v) the Respondent's written statement or submissions (if any) are considered by the Decision Maker.

**14A.4 Investigating and Determining the Dispute**

(a) The Society must, as soon as is reasonably practicable after receiving or becoming aware of a complaint made in accordance with this Constitution, ensure that the dispute is investigated and determined.

(b) Disputes must be dealt with under this Constitution in a fair, efficient, and effective manner.

**14A.5 Society May Decide Not to Proceed**

Despite Rule 14A.4, the Society may decide not to proceed further with a complaint if:

(a) the complaint is trivial;

(b) the complaint does not appear to disclose or involve any allegation of the following kind:

&emsp;(i) that a member or an Officer has engaged in material misconduct;

&emsp;(ii) that a member, an Officer, or the Society has materially breached, or is likely to materially breach, a duty under this Constitution, any bylaws, or the Incorporated Societies Act 2022;

&emsp;(iii) that a member's rights or interests, or members' rights or interests generally, have been materially damaged;

(c) the complaint appears to be without foundation or there is no apparent evidence to support it;

(d) the Complainant has an insignificant interest in the matter;

(e) the conduct, incident, event, or issue giving rise to the complaint has already been investigated and dealt with under this Constitution; or

(f) there has been an undue delay in making the complaint.

**14A.6 Referral of Complaint**

(a) The Society may refer a complaint to:

&emsp;(i) a subcommittee or an external person to investigate and report; or

&emsp;(ii) a subcommittee, an arbitral tribunal, or an external person to investigate and make a decision.

(b) The Society may, with the consent of all parties to a complaint, refer the complaint to any type of consensual dispute resolution (for example, mediation, facilitation, or a tikanga-based practice).

**14A.7 Decision Maker Impartiality**

(a) In this Section, "Decision Maker" means the person or persons who investigate and determine a complaint, whether the Committee, a subcommittee, an arbitral tribunal, or an external person.

(b) A person may not act as a Decision Maker in relation to a complaint if two (2) or more members of the Committee (or, if the complaint has been referred to a complaints subcommittee, two (2) or more members of that subcommittee) consider that there are reasonable grounds to believe that the person may not be:

&emsp;(i) impartial; or

&emsp;(ii) able to consider the matter without a predetermined view.

**14A.8 Outcomes**

(a) If a complaint is upheld, the Decision Maker may impose any one or more of the following outcomes:

&emsp;(i) require the Respondent to apologise;

&emsp;(ii) require the Respondent to undertake training or counselling;

&emsp;(iii) censure the Respondent;

&emsp;(iv) suspend the Respondent's membership or office for a specified period;

&emsp;(v) remove the Respondent from office;

&emsp;(vi) terminate the Respondent's membership (subject to Rule 14); or

&emsp;(vii) any other outcome the Decision Maker considers just and reasonable in the circumstances.

(b) The Decision Maker must give written notice of the decision and reasons to both the Complainant and the Respondent.

## [AMENDED] Section 26 — Winding Up

*This section is amended to update legislative references from the Incorporated Societies Act 1908 to the Incorporated Societies Act 2022, and to include the mandatory requirement for surplus asset distribution to a not-for-profit entity.*

**26.0** The Association may be wound up, dissolved, or removed from the register in accordance with the Incorporated Societies Act 2022.

**26.1** If, upon the winding up, dissolution, or removal from the register of the Association, there remain after the satisfaction of all its debts and liabilities any surplus assets, those surplus assets shall not be distributed to or among the members of the Association, but shall be given or transferred to one or more not-for-profit organisations having purposes similar to those of the Association, as determined by resolution at a General Meeting, or, if no such resolution is passed, as directed by the High Court pursuant to the Incorporated Societies Act 2022.

**26.2** For the avoidance of doubt, no member of the Association shall have any right or interest in the property of the Association, other than by way of a benefit authorised by the Act or this Constitution.

## [NEW SECTION] Section 27 — Common Seal

*This section clarifies that the Society does not use a common seal. The Incorporated Societies Act 2022 does not require a common seal (unlike the 1908 Act).*

The Society does not have a common seal. Documents may be executed by the signatures of two (2) Officers authorised by the Committee.

## [NEW SECTION] Section 28 — Amendments to the Constitution

*This new section is required to comply with section 26(1)(k) of the Incorporated Societies Act 2022, which requires the constitution to set out the method by which the constitution may be amended. See also sections 30–34 of the Act.*

**28.1** This Constitution may be amended by a resolution passed by a two‑thirds (⅔) majority of members present and voting at a General Meeting.

**28.2** Written notice of the proposed amendment must be given to all members at least fourteen (14) days before the meeting.

**28.3** Any amendment takes effect only when registered by the Registrar of Incorporated Societies, in accordance with sections 33–34 of the Act.

## Implementation

   - Put the amendments to a vote at a General Meeting in accordance with the existing constitutional amendment procedures; and
   - Submit the amended Constitution, together with any other required changes, to the Registrar of Incorporated Societies as part of the re-registration process.
